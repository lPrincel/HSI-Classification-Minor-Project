\begin{abstract}
Hyperspectral Image (HSI) classification poses significant computational and structural challenges due to the combination of high-dimensional spectral signatures, high spatial correlation, and severe training sample scarcity (the few-shot regime). While classical Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have long served as baselines, they struggle to model long-range spectral dependencies. Recent years witnessed the rise of Vision Transformers (ViTs); however, their quadratic self-attention complexity $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ presents severe memory bottlenecks on multi-spectral scenes. The year 2026 marks a paradigm shift with the introduction of cutting-edge Macro-Micro Vision Transformers (\texttt{MMFormer}) and Route-Reliability State Space Models (\texttt{R2Mamba}). In this work, we conduct a rigorous head-to-head empirical benchmark comparing \texttt{MMFormer} (\textit{IEEE TGRS 2026}) and \texttt{R2Mamba} (\textit{IEEE JSTARS 2026}) under an identical experimental protocol across two premier benchmarks: Indian Pines (NASA AVIRIS) and Pavia University (DLR ROSIS). Evaluated over three independent seeded runs to guarantee statistical reproducibility, our empirical results demonstrate that \texttt{R2Mamba} significantly outperforms \texttt{MMFormer}. On Indian Pines (16 classes), \texttt{R2Mamba} achieves $83.89\% \pm 1.66\%$ Overall Accuracy (OA) vs. $76.41\% \pm 3.84\%$ for \texttt{MMFormer} ($+7.48\%$ gain), while executing $2.9\times$ faster ($\sim$26s vs. $\sim$75s per run). On Pavia University (9 classes), \texttt{R2Mamba} attains $97.59\% \pm 0.60\%$ OA (peak $98.03\%$) compared to $92.45\% \pm 5.98\%$ for \texttt{MMFormer} ($+5.14\%$ gain), with $10\times$ lower variance and a $3.5\times$ training speedup ($\sim$40s vs. $\sim$140s per run). These findings confirm that selective State Space Models with linear complexity $\mathcal{O}(N)$ provide superior discriminative capability, convergence stability, and computational scalability over attention-based transformers in small-sample remote sensing domains.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Remote sensing hyperspectral imaging (HSI) captures radiant energy across hundreds of narrow, contiguous spectral bands spanning the visible, near-infrared (NIR), and short-wave infrared (SWIR) wavelengths. Unlike conventional RGB imagery that records only three broad color channels, each pixel in an HSI scene forms a continuous spectral reflectance curve---effectively acting as a unique diagnostic ``fingerprint'' that identifies surface materials based on molecular and atomic absorption characteristics.

Hyperspectral image classification---assigning each pixel to a land-cover or material class---serves as the foundational analysis step for vital real-world tasks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Precision Agriculture:} Monitoring crop vigor, identifying crop strains, detecting early weed infestations, and measuring soil nitrogen/moisture gradients.
    \item \textbf{Urban Planning:} Segmenting impervious surfaces, differentiating asphalt compositions, distinguishing metallic vs. tiled roofing, and cataloging urban tree canopies.
    \item \textbf{Environmental Science:} Mapping mineral deposits, monitoring forest degradation, tracking coastal algal blooms, and planetary surface geology.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Problem Statement \& Challenges}
Despite its capabilities, HSI classification encounters three fundamental bottlenecks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Hughes Phenomenon:} With 100 to 200+ raw spectral bands exhibiting strong cross-band correlation, the parameter space grows exponentially, leading to severe overfitting when training data is scarce.
    \item \textbf{Few-Shot Label Scarcity:} Ground-truth collection requires exhaustive on-site physical field surveys (ground truthing). Consequently, practical datasets contain very few labeled pixels per class (often only 10 to 30 training samples).
    \item \textbf{Computational Complexity Bottleneck:} Standard Vision Transformers compute all-to-all self-attention, whose time and memory complexity scales quadratically $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ with the number of tokens. On multi-megapixel remote sensing scenes, this results in extreme memory overhead and slow training latency.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Project Objectives \& Contributions}
The primary contributions of this project are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We establish a modular, reproducible PyTorch benchmarking framework for hyperspectral image classification.
    \item We conduct a two-phase investigation: Phase 1 validates the pipeline with five representative historical baselines (\texttt{HybridSN}, \texttt{SSRN}, \texttt{pResNet}, \texttt{DBDA}, and \texttt{SpectralFormer}), while Phase 2 conducts a direct empirical investigation of the two newest 2026 architectures: \texttt{MMFormer} (Vision Transformer) versus \texttt{R2Mamba} (State Space Model).
    \item All evaluations are performed under identical preprocessing (PCA to 30 bands), fixed spatial patch windows ($11 \times 11$), identical few-shot sample splits, and three seeded runs to report genuine statistical variance ($\text{Mean} \pm \text{Std}$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
The architectural paradigm for hyperspectral classification has progressed through three distinct eras:

\subsection{The Convolutional Era (2017--2020)}
Early deep learning approaches applied 2D and 3D Convolutional Neural Networks on dimensionality-reduced HSI data. Zhong \textit{et al.} introduced SSRN \cite{ssrn}, segregating 3D spectral convolutions from 3D spatial convolutions with residual connections. Roy \textit{et al.} proposed HybridSN \cite{hybridsn}, combining 3D convolutions for low-level spectral-spatial feature extraction with 2D convolutions for higher-level spatial abstraction. While efficient, CNNs inherently rely on local inductive biases and struggle to model spectral teleconnections across non-adjacent bands.

\subsection{The Transformer Era (2021--2024)}
Inspired by ViT, Hong \textit{et al.} proposed SpectralFormer \cite{spectralformer}, pioneering token-based spectral sequence modeling. Subsequent works such as MorphFormer and MASSFormer introduced memory-augmented and morphological tokens. However, standard multi-head self-attention computes an $N \times N$ attention matrix for $N$ spatial-spectral tokens:
\begin{equation}
\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V
\end{equation}
This quadratic cost ($\mathcal{O}(N^2)$) severely degrades training and inference throughput on large remote sensing spatial grids.

\subsection{The Mamba / State Space Revolution (2024--2026)}
To overcome the quadratic wall of Transformers while preserving global context modeling, Gu and Dao formulated Mamba \cite{mamba} using Selective State Space Models. By parameterizing the continuous linear time-invariant (LTI) system:
\begin{equation}
h'(t) = Ah(t) + Bx(t), \quad y(t) = Ch(t)
\end{equation}
and discretizing it into input-dependent selective recurrence, Mamba achieves strict linear complexity $\mathcal{O}(N)$ with hardware-aware parallel scans. In remote sensing, MambaHSI and R2Mamba \cite{r2mamba} adapted this framework to multidirectional spatial-spectral scanning, demonstrating remarkable efficiency.

\section{Methodology \& Model Formulations}

\subsection{End-to-End Pipeline}
The benchmarking pipeline follows a standardized workflow:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dimensionality Reduction:} Principal Component Analysis (PCA) reduces raw bands (up to 200) to 30 principal components, capturing $>99\%$ of spectral variance while eliminating sensor noise.
    \item \textbf{On-the-Fly Patch Extraction:} Spatial patches of size $11 \times 11$ centered on labeled pixels are extracted dynamically by PyTorch \texttt{DataLoader} instances, preventing RAM exhaustion.
    \item \textbf{Few-Shot Split Protocol:} Strict class-balanced sample partitioning with per-run seeds (\texttt{seeds: [1, 2, 3]}).
    \item \textbf{Model Training:} Supervised cross-entropy minimization with Adam optimizer ($\text{lr}=0.001$, batch size 64) and early stopping (patience 12 epochs).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{MMFormer Architecture}
Published in \textit{IEEE TGRS} in 2026, \texttt{MMFormer} \cite{mmformer} addresses spatial scale disparity via a dual-branch architecture:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Macro Branch:} Downsamples the input patch into coarse semantic tokens, allowing the network to capture global scene context and regional field geometry.
    \item \textbf{Micro Branch:} Operates at fine resolution to preserve high-frequency spectral transitions and subtle boundary textures.
    \item \textbf{Interactive Cross-Attention:} A cross-scale attention bridge dynamically aligns macro contextual features with micro spectral tokens. Its asymptotic complexity remains quadratic: $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{R2Mamba Architecture}
Published in \textit{IEEE JSTARS} in 2026, \texttt{R2Mamba} \cite{r2mamba} introduces Route-Reliability selective scanning:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bidirectional Spatial-Spectral Scanning:} Flattens 3D patches into sequence tokens scanned across multiple spatial directions to resolve Mamba's 1D directional bias.
    \item \textbf{Route Reliability Gating (RRG):} At boundary transitions between disparate land-cover classes, mixed pixels degrade scanning quality. R2Mamba dynamically computes a reliability score $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ for each scanning route, downweighting ambiguous trajectories before recurrent state pooling.
    \item \textbf{Complexity:} Operates with strict linear complexity: $\mathcal{O}(N)$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Results \& Discussion}

\subsection{Benchmark Datasets}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Indian Pines (AVIRIS):} $145 \times 145$ pixels, 200 raw bands (PCA $\rightarrow$ 30), 16 agricultural crop classes, $20\text{ m}$ spatial resolution. Severe class imbalance (classes from 20 to 2,455 pixels). Sample budget: 10 train / 5 validation pixels per class; 9,482 test pixels.
    \item \textbf{Pavia University (ROSIS):} $610 \times 340$ pixels, 103 raw bands (PCA $\rightarrow$ 30), 9 urban land-cover classes, $1.3\text{ m}$ spatial resolution. Sample budget: 30 train / 10 validation pixels per class; 40,044 test pixels.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Discussion & Complexity Analysis}
\textbf{Accuracy & Stability:} \texttt{R2Mamba} establishes clear dominance across all metrics. On Indian Pines, \texttt{R2Mamba} achieves an OA of $83.89\%$, outperforming \texttt{MMFormer} by $+7.48\%$ while exhibiting $2.3\times$ lower standard deviation ($\pm 1.66\%$ vs. $\pm 3.84\%$). On Pavia University, \texttt{R2Mamba} reaches an exceptional $97.59\%$ mean OA ($98.03\%$ peak) with near-zero variance ($\pm 0.60\%$). \texttt{MMFormer} suffers from noticeable instability on urban boundaries ($\pm 5.98\%$ OA variance) due to quadratic token sensitivity.

\textbf{Computational Efficiency:} As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, \texttt{R2Mamba} trains in $\sim$26 seconds on Indian Pines and $\sim$40 seconds on Pavia University, representing a \textbf{2.9$\times$ to 3.5$\times$ speedup} over \texttt{MMFormer}. Because \texttt{R2Mamba} computes linear $\mathcal{O}(N)$ selective recurrent states, its memory consumption remains constant per token step, allowing seamless scaling to larger remote sensing scenes without GPU memory overflow.

\section{Conclusion}
This project presented a rigorous empirical head-to-head evaluation of the two latest 2026 hyperspectral classification architectures: \texttt{MMFormer} and \texttt{R2Mamba}. Our findings confirm that selective State Space Models resolve the fundamental limitations of Vision Transformers in remote sensing: delivering higher accuracy ($+5\%$ to $+7.5\%$ OA), substantially lower variance, and a $3.5\times$ reduction in training latency.
